# Reflection: Ethics, Privacy and Emerging Tech

## 1. Data Privacy Act of 2012
Under the Data Privacy Act of 2012, the online seller must secure explicit consent from customers before collecting and processing their personal data, establish lawful processing criteria, and enforce strict security measures. To protect user privacy during analytics, columns containing direct identifiers such as names, precise delivery addresses, and phone numbers must be completely removed or masked.

## 2. COD Cancellation Impact
Discontinuing Cash-on-Delivery (COD) based solely on high cancellation rates would unfairly penalize unbanked customers or buyers with limited digital payment access who rely on COD for trusted transactions. Instead of a blanket ban, the seller should decide by weighing the logistics cost of cancellations against the lost market share of unbanked buyers, perhaps introducing partial deposits or verified user incentives instead.

## 3. Five Vs of Big Data
- **Volume:** Appears in the generation and processing of 1,000,000 individual online sale orders[cite: 3].
- **Velocity:** Appears in the massive transaction traffic spikes concentrated between 12:00 to 1:00 AM and 8:00 to 10:00 PM on November 11[cite: 3, 4].

## 4. Blockchain Integration
Integrating a blockchain-backed ledger could securely track immutable supply chain and delivery handoff milestones for each order, ensuring tamper-proof auditing across third-party logistics partners. However, a major drawback is the significant overhead in transaction latency, throughput limitations, and infrastructure complexity relative to traditional databases.
